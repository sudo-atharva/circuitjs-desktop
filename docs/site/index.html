Draw a circuit. Watch it run. Probe it like a bench.

A free circuit simulator for Windows, based on CircuitJS1. Real-time animation, oscilloscope probes, a big waveform viewer with cursors and slope, a programmable MCU, 74HC595 / 74HC165 chips, timed power supplies, and datasheet-style PDF export. About 7 MB.

Download for Windows Example circuits

Windows 10 / 11. Installer or portable circuitjs.exe. Not code-signed yet: if SmartScreen warns, click More info → Run anyway.

What you can do

Probe anything

Press h, click a wire: a colored scope channel. Drag probes around while the circuit runs.

Measure like a scope

Peak-to-peak, RMS, frequency, duty, rise/fall time, slew rate, and slope between two cursors.

Program an MCU

An 8-pin chip that runs a small Arduino-like program: blink, read buttons and knobs, bit-bang shift registers.

Shift registers

74HC595 (latch, tri-state, chainable) and 74HC165 with their real pins: 8 outputs or 8 inputs over 3 wires.

Timed power supply

24 V after 1 s, on/off cycles, or a PLC-style timer relay that follows a trigger with on and off delays.

Everyday circuits

Transistor switches, optocoupler inputs, short-circuit protection, regulators, 555: ready to open and change.

Datasheet PDF

One click: an A4 datasheet with schematic, measured characteristics, waveforms, BOM and conditions.

AI control (MCP)

Let Claude or another AI build and simulate circuits in the running app. Cheap, line-by-line edits.

Get started in a minute

1. Open an example

Circuits → Everyday Circuits, or browse the gallery. It starts running at once.

2. Hover and click

Hover a part for its voltage and current; click switches; double-click a part to change its value.

3. Look at the signal

Press h and click a wire, or double-click a small graph: the Waveform Viewer opens with cursors.

4. Build your own

Draw menu or a key (r resistor, w wire, g ground...), then drag. More in the guide.
